# sites

A site is a World's website (step 78): its pages, the component at the top and the foot of every page, redirects and files, kept as a draft until the World's founder or an admin publishes it. Look first (artifact_list kind site): a World has one site. site_create makes one, private to the person. Its address is in what site_create returns and in site_read: a free one, <its name>.neucleos.site, where Neucleos gives one at once, or a hostname Neucleos's operators add; a site with no address cannot be published. Write it whole with site_write in its format, one block per entry, each closed by "@@end": "@@site" (language:, theme: the phone's bar color, icon: and image: paths of its files); "@@style" (CSS for every page: Tailwind's @theme with the World's colors and fonts, @font-face for its own font files, and rules of its own; sections' Tailwind classes are compiled with it when it is built); "@@nav <component>" and "@@foot <component>" (a widget from the catalog with one "key: value" line per input, as on a canvas; a widget with an input "path" is given each page's route, to mark where the visitor is); "@@page /route" (title:, description:, image: the preview picture's path, and canvas: <a canvas's name>, made empty if the World has none of that name, or html: <an HTML file's name> for a page as it is); "@@redirect /old" (to: a route or an https address, status: 308 unless said); "@@file /images/team.webp" (media: a file of the World's, by name, added with media_add). Each route once; nothing is written if a route is named twice, a component does not exist, or a file is not this World's. A page's words are its canvas (guide: canvases): a section is a "@@component" block whose inputs are its words, so changing words never touches code; text, pictures and tables are blocks too. Sections are widgets made for a website: full width, styled with Tailwind classes or the roles, taking their words and pictures (a file's path, like /images/team.webp) as inputs, never fetching anything; they may import the pack (motion, three.js, lucide-react's icons). A site's form posts JSON (name, email, phone, message, service, page, requestKey) to /api/website-inquiry and reads { receiptKey } or { error }: it lands in the World's Website messages and the founder's bell. site_read gives the site in its format, each page's canvas, what is live, and what people changed since you last read, on the site and on its pages; read before you write. Nothing goes live until it is published: in the site's tab the founder or an admin builds it (each section drawn once, every page's HTML, a private preview at phone and desktop widths), sees what changes against the live site, and publishes exactly that. You cannot publish: site_publish asks them, in their bell; say so.
