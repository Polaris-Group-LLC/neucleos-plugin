# brands

A brand is how a World looks and writes: its colors, type, shapes, logos, the patterns its pages repeat and its voice, each entry saying how sure it is and where it came from. Look first (search, artifact_list): extend the World's brand rather than make a second. Make or change one with brand_create, brand_read and brand_write, in its one format. brand_create can begin from a starter, a small whole brand in two themes with every entry proposed: Editorial (warm paper, ink and a serif, for a company that writes), Product (crisp, one sans, one blue, for software), Playful (cream, plum and tangerine, round, for a shop or a class), or Neucleos (its own look). Outside blocks, the brand book: a "# Name" line, a "themes:" line ("themes: day (light), night (dark)"; the first theme is the one a value falls back to), an optional "fonts:" line in words, then a paragraph or three of what the brand is. Then one block per entry: "@@color tomato", "@@type display", "@@space step-4", "@@radius", "@@shadow", "@@gradient", "@@motion", "@@layout", "@@pattern Price tag", "@@voice Stance", "@@imagery Photography", "@@logo Stamp", "@@surface Website", "@@rule", each closed by "@@end". Inside, "key: value" lines (several may share a line, joined by " · "): a value per theme where it differs ("day: #c8341f", "night: #ff6a4d"; or "value:"), "role:" where the entry fills one of the roles every World has (text, muted, faint, ground, surface, surface-2, line, line-2, accent, on-accent, good, warn, bad, focus, radius, font, display, mono; each once), "usage:", "avoid:", "on:", "sure:" (exact: read from a source file; measured: computed from pictures of pages; estimated: judged by eye; proposed: made up for what the sources never had; per theme when they differ, "sure: night exact · day proposed"), "from:" (the pages or files it came from), and for a type style "font:" (family and weight), "size:", "tracking:", "case:", "sample:"; for a logo "media:" (its file's name in the World's Files: add it with media_add first, copied from the sources, never drawn), "ink:", "min:", "clear:"; for a pattern "component:" (the widget that draws it). A color is #hex, rgb(), oklch() or another color by name in braces ("{violet}"). Color, type, space, radius, shadow, gradient, motion and layout names are lowercase letters, digits and hyphens; the rest are plain words. A pattern, voice, imagery, surface or rule says what it is in its body, in Markdown: a pattern's anatomy, when to use it and when not, and the pages it came from; a voice from the founder's own words, quoted, with what to avoid, and a register per surface. Never guess a value and mark it exact. brand_read lists contrast under 4.5:1 per theme (3:1 for lines that carry meaning and focus rings): keep a source's own colors and say so; propose an added theme rather than retint. A brand needs text, ground and accent roles before it can be released. From a PDF (a deck, a brand book): source_add it, then brand_extract: say its price first, and with the person's yes it reads the PDF's fill colors, fonts and pictures into a draft brand, each value exact with how often it was used, the roles a first guess to check with them. Fonts: file_upload with for: font and the brand, then brand_font_add with its licence in words; only a licence that allows the web is served on public pages. People change values and approve entries in its tab; brand_read names what they changed. Release it with brand_release only when the person says so (only the person who made it can): a release never changes, editing changes only the draft, and it says how many entries are still proposed and not approved. Before a release, a proof of the person's own (a page, a desktop) wears the draft with brand_use (draft: true), following each change, so you both see the brand at work. To review it with them, a desktop list "@@list Review · from brand <its name>" shows its proposed and estimated entries, live, with what each is and where it came from; their tick approves one in the brand's history, under their name. With nothing to start from, a workshop: three directions on a desktop, each a "Brand direction" widget ("name", "colors" as "ink #1d232b, paper #fbfaf7, …", "display" and "text" as Google Fonts with a weight, "voice"), beside a poll of the three; the chosen one becomes a small first brand, every entry proposed, from the starter nearest it. brand_files gives a release's files: brand.md and tokens.json to read, or to make a Claude Design System from; brand.css, a Tailwind preset, an Office theme (theme.xml), the fonts and the logos, for a site, a deck or a document made elsewhere. From a Claude Design System's files, brand_import makes the brand (its logos in the World's Files first; its fonts after). The World's founder or an admin chooses the World's brand on the World page, and then everything in the World wears its newest release, at each thing's next load: widgets and apps (their pages get the brand's variables, fonts and ground), pages (canvases, in their own box), and public pages and embeds (its ground, fonts and logo); the cockpit around them stays Neucleos. brand_use makes one thing wear another of the World's brands, none, or another theme (a page in its light theme). So write widgets with the roles only (var(--n-text), var(--n-accent), var(--n-ground), var(--n-on-accent), var(--n-font)…), never a brand's literal colors, and they fit every World; a brand's own color is var(--b-<name>, var(--n-<role>)). useBrand() gives a widget the brand's values and logos (guide: components). For what Neucleos does not dress (a file made elsewhere), read the brand first and use its colors, type and voice. An agent with brandVoice: true writes in the brand's voice where it runs (its voice entries, the public register for visitors, none of its avoided words); a picture agent in a branded World follows its imagery entries unless the request says otherwise. A page (canvas) presents its sections as 16:9 slides in its brand (Present) and prints to a PDF, Letter or A4, with its title in the footer and its page numbers (Download PDF), in its tab and on its public page.
